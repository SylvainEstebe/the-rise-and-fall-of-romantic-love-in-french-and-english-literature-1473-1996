A Discourse between Philip Herbert and Charon, at the River of Acheron, and in his Voyage to the Port of Hell. Enter Philip Herbert, at the side of Acheron, calling Charon. Philip.SO Ho, so ho! Zbood Come Charon, come away, delay me not, I haste to bee at the Port, where I May keep my brother-Rebells company. Charon.Feare not, brave Champion, thou shalt have no delay; my Boat 's both rig'd and trim'd, Ile quickly waft thee. Ph.O come prithee, Dam mee, the time passeth away very swift; Let mee not lose this Tyde. Cha.Ile not neglect thee, No, without all doubt, thou wilt find the time long enough, 'Tis to Eternity, when once th ' art in Thou wilt be kept close-Prisoner for thy Sin. Ph.Eternity! Sin! Zbood what are those? I know them not. Sinke me! Zounds! can a Parliament man commit Sin? No, no sure hee cannot. Cha,Your Lordship is mistaken in Parliament men, Who, as the Case now stands Act, altogether, against God's-Commands. Ph.Judge me! Like enough! for I never thought three times of God these eight yeeres,Image 003-b Nor did I ever think there was a need Remember God, or to repeat my Creed. Cha.I know it Sir, For which, Hell is prepar'd and, you must frie In Sulphry flames, for Treas'n & Perjurie. Ph.Rot mee, if your sport bee so hot Ile goe from whence I came, I like it not. Cha.No Sir; it is too late to Repent, I have obey'd your Call And you must goe; it is in vain at all T' attempt Escape, I 've Legions at Command, Who soon will seiz, and bind you foot and hand. Ph.A Pox on't, Ile pay thee well if thou wilt let mee Escape and goe back to Whitehall For Truth I find That Hell will both Torment Body and Mind. One foote in the Boate he leapt back again on Shoare. Cha.'Tis true indeed Sir, and you must Have your Deserts, your punishment is just; Then step into the Boat, I cannot stay, Some other Fare will come, Let's haste away. Ph.Zounds, why so hastie? Stay a little; though I was in hast at the first and ran headlong, now( perceiving what I must suffer) I can well spare a little time. Cha.Yes, but the time's ore-past and th' Furies houle To be a gnawing thy Blood-stayned-Soule, There's no remedy then, nor can I so Much freedom grant that back again thou goe. He leaps into the Boate. Ph.A Plague on thee then, Take me to the Devill and spare not, I hope I shall be Comrade with Dorislaus. My Misery will be abated, some Reliefe 'T will be to have Co-partners in my Griefe. Cha.Wee shall be there quickly Sir, I have no more to doe but only to deliver you over to Cerberus, Then get of him what Terms you can or may. You will not long, I'm sure, for Sentence stay. Ph.May! Zoood What May dost thou meane? If my deere Mistres the Lady May be here To burne in thousand Hells I will not feare. Image 004-aCha.No, no, it is not shee by may I meane; get what freedom you can untill your Arraignment and Triall be past, 'tis that I meane. Ph.The Devill take thee! Judge me! I had strong hopes the Lady May had been before To keep mee Company and bee my Wh— . Cha.There are Hounds( and please you Sir plenty) which will barke and baule to purpose when your Lordship comes a little neerer. Ph.Dam mee, I like the noyse deerely; I alwaies lov'd Hunting well: But preethee what stinke of Brimstone and Burning is this? Sure 'ts not the constant smell Which doth arise from the Abysse of Hell. Cha.No my Lord, this is but small to the stink when you are in; But hold now Cerberus barks, wee must leave talking. Ph.Sinke mee, there's a Damn'D noyse indeed; If this bee a Hell-hound I like not the Musick. Enter Cerberus opening Acheruse. Cer.O Charon, who is there? What hast thou brought? Is that an English Statesman thou hast caught? Cha.I hee 's come from England, his name was Pembroke, one of our chiefe Champions: For Damming, Sinking, Swearing & Cursing, all the Inhabitants of Hell can hardly equal him. Cerb.Well then Charon on thy report Ile entertain him, If 't bee Herbert, I have waited for him these many yeeres. Farewell Charon, help mee with some more, I fain would fill my Goale with English Store. Cha.Adieu my Lord. Pem.Zblood art thou gone? Farwell and be hang'd. Cer,Come, walk with mee, and Ile thy Lordship bring Where Judas is, that first betray'd his King. Pem.With all my heart, I ' de fain shake hands with him. He draws neere and the Damned houle. Dam me! What noyse is this? fough they stinke, is Atkins here, or have they beshit themselves? A pox take um they'll Poyson mee, I'le not goe amongst them; this place is much too hot, I'le have a cooler if it can bee got. Cer.No such matter Sir, This was provided for you, and this place Image 004-b Must bee your lodging, till great Pluto's Grace Appoint your Triall, which will bee to morrow, And then your Doom will bee eternall-Sorrow. Pem.The Devill grace him, if it must be so, it shall be so; Oh, oh▪ oh! Zblood, Zounds, Dam mee and Sink me! Woe, woe, woe, woe, My Pains begin, I feele the Torment due unto my Sin. Enter Pym, Hamden, two Hothams, Essex, Raynsborough, Dorislaus, Brookes, Jones. &c. all houling & wayling. Pem.Zblood whats the matter men, all mad? The Devill 's in you I think, no better welcome? All weeping and mourning as black as Hell. All the Reb.Best welcom we can give to thee 's to tell Thou must tormented bee with us in Hell. Pem,You doe not know that till I have been Tried. All.That will bee quickly done. Pem.May I chuse Councell? All.Yes, yes, You may. Pem.Dam-me then, because they know the Lawes, My Councell shall bee Pim and Dorislaus. The three Judges being set Gerberus comes with the black-guard to carry Pembroke to the Barre. Enter Aeachus, Minos and Raddamanthus in Chayres. Pembrokes Charge is read. Clarke.You Philip Harbert once Earle of Pembroke, but late one of the Commons of Westminster, stand charged of these following Crimes and Inormities: Perjury, Treason, Murther, Adultery, Blasphemy, Swearing, Cursing, Fornication, Rebellion, Sacriledge, Tyrannie, with innumerable more of the like nature, as Drunkenesse, &c. whether are you guilty or not guilty? what can you say for your selfe, why you should not suffer eternall punishment amongst your brothers, Pim. Hambden, Brooks. &c. What say you? guilty, or not guilty? Pem.' Zlood I cannot plead, will you allow mee Councell? Judg.Heer's no Councell, here we are both Judg and Councell; Plead quickly, or wee proceed to Sentence, what say'st thou? Pem.Judge me: Sink me I know not what to say more, but that I alwayes did what your spirit of wickednesse prompted. I think I've beene Hells friend indeede. Nor can you justly say, That e're from Sathans Law I turn'd away. Judges.'Tis true, therefore receive thy pay.Image 005-a Raddamanthus passeth sentence and commits Pembroke to Megaera, Alecto, and Tesiphone. Radda.By the power of Lucifer great Prince of this infernall Abysse, &c. you are to take the Soule of Philip Harbert prisoner at the Barre, and dispose it into Lethe, first Carbona doing and broyling him upon the Coales, and then in the coldest Lake tumble him; yet not ceasing, give him boyling lead for drink, with all the greatest tortures that Hell can imagine, thus using your greatest skill in tormenting him, let not his misery cease to eternity, as you tender the displeasure of your chiefe Prince and Ruler. The three furies lay hold on him, and hale him head-long into Hell fire. Hee houles and cryes, curses and sweares, Whil'st his tormentors rayne infernall snares Upon him, making him to quaffe Full boules oth' Stigian-Lake, whil'st they doe laugh At his Damnation, and are glad That in their hands they've got the dam-me Lad. The Epilogue. PEMBROCK in Hell, with grievous Sighs and Groanes Doth Eccho forth eternall Woes and Moanes Unto the Furies, and with gummy teares Attempts to penetrate their deafned eares; But, all in vain, they stop not to torment him, It was too late( when once in Hell) Repent him; Nor can hee e're get out, eternally Within those Sulph'ry-flames his Soule must fry; If all our Regicides were with him there, Thrice happy, happy, then this England were.
