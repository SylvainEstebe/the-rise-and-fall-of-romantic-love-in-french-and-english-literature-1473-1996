HOLLANDS LEAGVER: OR, AN HISTORICAL Discourse of the Life and Actions, of Dona Britanica Hollandia, the Arch-Mistris of the wicked Women of Eutopia. THis History, Tale, or tell-troth, (take it in which sence you please) is dedicated to the World, whither all discourses wander, to the reformation of vice, which is Good mens wishes, and to the aduancement of vertue, which is the ayme of all honest affections [Page]so that making my summe iust with mine account, I doubt not but I shall discharge my selfe, of that which I owe to posteritie, to the ruine of sinne, and the immortall safe-guarde of piety. But you will tell me, to detect sinne is to teach sinne, the discouery of vice like Hydras heads, doth rather increase then decrease the vitious, for vertue is seldome found to spring from Lacedemonian Tables, and chastity much lesse from Aretines pictures; that wicked persons, and wicked actions, should rather bee damn'd in obscuritie, then preserued for eternity, that it is a sinne against piety, to giue wickednesse any life of memory, for so the villaine became lost that set fire on Diana's Temple, and Manlius was forgot in Rome, that did iniury to Rome: wicked names dishonour fayre report, and if by compulsion the pen must glance vpon them, they ought euer to be accompanied with curses, and execrations; vnrepentant sinne is the Deuils Godsonne, and when we talke of capital wickednes, we ought to giue it no other name but deuill; [Page]Yet Brutus and his confederates are not forgot in Liuie; Sinon liues in Virgil, and Pandarus in Homer, we haue a Layis memorable in Corinth, a Lamia in Athens, and a Lestrigon in great Brittaine, why should we then loose this Eutopian Legend of Britanica Hollandia, who exceeds all in insolence, instructs all in impudence, and runnes through a World of damnable actions, with improuidence; no, as she is without paralell in her profession, so I wil striue to delineate her to the life, that she may euer liue hatefull to all after succession. In the Kingdome of Eutopia, nere the fayre citie of Maugsun, some fewe Leagues distant, their liued a Gentleman of indifferent Fortunes, neither so high that ambition did assaile him, nor so low that contempt might follow him, but Anchoring in a smooth low tyde, and a safe harbor, made himselfe content only with things necessary; he marryed a wife sutable to his estate, and agreeable with his affections, for shee had comelinesse enough to content an honest eye; cleanlines sufficient for a chast bed, or a frugall Table, and discretion at will [Page]to gouerne and dispose all things committed to her Steward-shippe; These had issue one daughter whom they named Britanica Hollandia, Hollandia her birth.by reason of some neere allyances betwixt them and the Neather-lands. A mayd in the time of her innocence, and ignorance, so accomplished and accomodated with all the ornaments of Nature and education, that shee farre surpassed all those of her rancke, and might without the offence of modesty challenge equalitie with those which were accounted excellent; for shee had a fayre Eye, full of strong inchauntment; a modest cheeke, rich with bashfulnesse; and a face so generally clothed with beauty, & blushes, that there was an infinite promise of much ensuing vertue: her stature was but low, yet compos'd with such true Cymetrie, and so agreeable with euery other lineament through the whole Fabricke of her body, that had Appelles beheld her, he would haue forsworn his Idol to haue made her his goddesse; but to leaue off all further circumstance, let it suffice me to [Page]say shee was truely handsome, exceeding hopefull, and had not the Deuill beene too ambitious, shee might have bin most happy, but where the house is cleanest, there Sathan desires to entersoonest; and if the guard of Grace be Idle or vnwatchfull, repentance may follow, but it cannot prevent surprizall, and so it fares with this Creature, for as heaven had bestowed vpon her all outward imbelishments to attract vertue vnto▪ her, so had some sinister power, or the corruption of her owne flesh married to those bodily beauties, such a wicked spirit, that all goodnes became to her an vtter stranger▪ shee was of such a proud, haughty, and disdainfull nature, so Masculine in her disposition, and so mad to encounter any crosse to her best loued cogitations, that like the Spider, which turneth all things to poyson which it tasteth, so she became an vtter Athiest, and would acknowledge no God but her own pleasure; She had from mens prayses, womens envies, and her glasses plain-dealing, her parents tendernesse, [Page]her friends carefulnes, and her companions amazednesse, got such a true knowlege of her handsomnesse, that she did not onely beleeue it, and loue it, but mightily doated vpon it; neither did this foolishnesse of selfe-affection so bind her to the admiration of her owne beautie, as a proud and furious conceire of the excellency of her wit, (aboue the sphere of other womens capacities) did bind and slaue her to imbrace to her selfe this conclusion that she only was excellent Now she begins to grow in hate with her fathers house, the Country is vnpleasant, vnhabitable, obscure and durtie, that house-wifely imployments, were slauish ingagements; to live with parents, was but to lye with bondage, that al comman dements were curses, and obedience only a figure of simplicitie, and ignorance; shee would often say within her selfe, what is beautie, if not seene, what seene, if not admired, what admired, if not desired, and what any, or all if not enioyed; and where shall these bee gathered from [Page]the blossomes of trees, heaps of stones the ouerflowes of fountains, or the dul rabble of Clownes and heards-men? No, it is the City that is the spheare of beauty, there are her Temples, there live her votaries, and there burnes her sacrifice, the Countrie is but a rotten chest that conceales holy reliques, let me live in the Citie, where if I cannot out-shine all, or at least equall all, yet a tworst I shall be hidde in an handsome Cabinet. Vpon these, and the like resolutions she throws her-selfe into a deepe melancholie: all meates are but course for her dyet, shee forsakes them; sleepe is too neere a kinseman to death, she hates it; all company seemes rude, shee loathes it; all discourses are vnpollish't, her eare will not receiue them; and to conclude, as an exanimated carcasse, she walks with no soule, and but halfe a motion. Her parents quickly perceiue this alteration, and like noble Phisitians labor to find out the cause to make the cure more easie, more speedie, more certaine; but she [Page]that had a lockt vp soule in a fort impregnable, what through disdaine, the handmaid of Pride, & Pride it selfe the Lord of stubborne resolution, she became so impenitrable, that sooner might they bring the Poles together, then separate her from one tittle of her private resolution, so that finding her fixed, they leaue to molest her, onely obserue all occasions, and all circumstances, euery passage, and euery Action, that might give any light to those designes on which shee laboured with such difficultie; in the end, finding her set one day in a priuate Roome, without any companion, but solitude & her owne thoughts, her Father stealing within the distance of her speeches, might heare her vtter these wordes. O life, which hast deceiued so many, seduced so many, blinded so many, thou art nothing at thy beginning, thy light is nothing but a shaddow, thou art but smoake at thy height, thou art sweet to fooles, thou art bitter to the wise, who loueth thee, knowes thee not, who knoweth thee, contemnes thee; and here with a deepe sigh shee [Page]made a full period, when her father pressing vpon her, demanded the cause of this quarrell betwixt her and life, vrging her with much ingratitude, to condemne that, from which her selfe had receiued so much luster, applying her beauty, her youth, her wit, and all her inward, and outward faculties, as vnrefellible arguments of infinite beholdingnesse to life, who had brought them into the World, & made them beloued of the World; but she with a silent bashfulnesse (fuller of deceit then innocence) forbearing to reply gave him occasion to assaile her with all the strength that nature, loue, & a fathers authority could collect together, and did so vrge her to display, and lay open the Cabinet of her deerest counsells, that she either vnwilling to loose so faire an opportunity, or to apparel her envious griefe in the robes of obedience, with a seeming vnwilling willingnesse, did disburden her heart of that loade of griefe, which so long had afflicted her: yet not without many disguises, and false habits, as [Page]not being weary of the Countrey for it's solitude, but carefull to discharge him of much cost, and incumbrance; that the City was more hopefull for her aduancement, because the two columbes of posteritie, honorable Mariage, and wealthy imployment had there a setled residence, and threw away their fauors blindfold, like the children of Fortune. There did need small debatement,Her comming to the City. When both the parties did agree vpon one proposition, for he seemed to outrun her in willingnes, and as if their thoughts had bene cast in one mould, an equall diligence made preparation for her speedy departure, the mothers consent was not wanting, so that all drawing by one linke without hinderance, strife or aduantage, the work was in a trice effected, the daughter was brought to the City, and setled in an honest Magnificos seruice. Here, after the expence of some small time, liuing as it were in a Burse of resort, where men and their minds were continually [Page]trucking for new commodities, it was impossible for this rich Iewell, which was without foyle; because it knew no Art, and without fraud by reason of an innocent ignorance, should either stand gaping for a chap-man, or like an idle prentise, with his hands hid in his cap, cry, Sir, what lacke you; No, vnsullied beauty like the Sunne will giue light to a whole Hemyspheare▪ and this damosell had so shot her beames through euery corner of the City, that her Suitors were like Coriscas smockes, infinite in number, many in good opinion, and but one at one time in imployment, yet with such seuerity she gouerned her passions, that the strictest eye of modesty could hardly detect her gouernment, and she was the rather induced to this Austerity, by beholding the pictures of two famous Curtezans, which did hang in her Masters Gallery; the first, which vvas lesse faire, but more fortunate, had this inscription, Si non Caste tamen Caute, the other which vvas farre beyond all for beauty, and inferior [Page]to none for Miserie, had vvritten ouer her. Nec caste, Nec caute; on these two plaine songs her resolution made sundry descants, but the close of euery strain was, That to sinne wisely, was to sinne safely, that after-thoughts vvere foolish, and had I wist childish; hence it came that amōgst the vn-numbred number of her Suitors, she chose one whom shee knevv honest,Her marriage. by all mens report, frugall in her owne experience, industrious in his affaires, and painefull in euery circumstance of his profession, one that was handsome enough to loue, and stout enough to controle, yet such an Idolater to her beauty, & such a slave to her commandements, that neither his eyes, nor eares could carry credit beyond her sufferance; of this man she made a husband, and had not the deuill and womans vveakenesse conspir'd to make ship vvracke of her vertue, they might have liued and dyed vvith faire reputation; but novv shee shakes handes vvith bashfulnes, and feare, & the manlinesse of her courage,Her falling from vertue. meeting vvith the libertie [Page]of a bold wives rudenes, she dare both intice and lend an eare to inticements. 'Tis true, that loue in her friends, made them call impudence an high spirit, and losenesse a pleasant behauiour, but those which had beene brought vp in the same schoole where shee learnt her complement, knew that the gate was wide enough, and the way plaine enough which leade to the ruine of her vertue. Many assault were giuen to this faire Fort,Her subtiltie. but some she with-stood, to gaine an opinion of goodnesse, and they were great ones; some she durst not entertaine for feare of after repentance, and they were loose ones, some she held at a distance to draw on benefit, and they were the rich ones, and some she scorn'd, and they were fooles, Fidlers, or base ones: But behold, at last, the deuill himselfe fearing to lose so rich a prize, which hee knew would not come to his kingdome alone with her owne lading, but with the spoyles, and triumphs of a world of vnfortunate creatures of both sexes, he sent to assaile her one Ignatius a Puritane Iesuite; The first that made her a whore.a fellow, whom Lucifer had so instructed, and adorned with all the deceiuable [Page]qualities and inchauntments of a seeming goodnesse, that vertue herselfe could hardly have discouered him, without the vse of her diuinest spectacles; he had al the amiablenesses of a good face, and a well-shap'd body, which he made more glorious with grauity & good cloathes, he had an excellent wit, prompt and ready, which he witnessed in his discourses, being full of delight and learning, he was full of Bounty, and liberalitie; a world of poore deceiued people (especially women) maintaining his exchequor, and when the fire of lust was kindled in him by the deuils bellowes, he was so full of delicate Petulance, and inchaunting dallyance, that a cold halfe buried Anchorite would haue ingendred a flame from his embers. This great General for Hel (not alone in this, but in a world of other damnable actions) com's with his Legions of inchantments to besiege this Redoute, or weake Sconce, euen at the time when there was a mutynie raised betwixt her and her best souldiers; for indeed, Modestie was in disgrace, and accus'd for a Coward, Chastity was scorn'd as a shaddow & no substance, [Page]Bashfulnesse was reputed weake and childish, Honour a Chimera, Good-fame a dreame; and indeed, all the Vertues stood cashired as enemies vnto Pleasure; so that there was left none to withstand him but Frailtie, a Cannon-law, and a Penall Statute. Against these hee rais'd such a battery of sweet discourse, and inchanting perswasions, that she stood amazed, and staggering at the first on-set, till feare of the Law, and a little loue to good report, stept betwixt her and the enemy, and beat him backe with these, or the like arguments. First, that her Reputation (not God or his Iudgements) were so deare and tender vnto her, that she durst not give eare to his sorceries: but he with a smile (wherein a good eye might haue discerned scorne) asked her what Reputation was; She told him it was the very Manna, and Nepenthe of generous Spirits, the Goddesse of great courages, and the triumph of all Noble Actions; she sayd it was a thing so delicate, so pure, and so vnspotted, that the least excesse did blemish it, any vniust action dishonour it, indiscretion, negligence or Idlenesse deface it, but Prostitution [Page]that did wholly ruine it; it is a constant friend that will follow and go with any good action, but if once forsaken, no inquisition, no search, no prayers, or teares, can euer againe find it: Beleeve it Sir, (said she) there is no such misery as to outlive Reputation, nor any follies like that vvhich puts it in hazard. But he, to whom Blasphemy was familiar, & Atheistical propositions more frequent, then holy sentences, had soone brought her to a Non-plus; so that forsaking her guardians, she vvas content to hearken to composition. This capitulation assuring him of future conquest, he now began to sing his owne triumph; and to make his conditions more glorious, the assurance of her affections more serious, and his laciuious pleasures, of a long liv'd indurance, he began to discourse vnto her the liues & Legends, (but not the deaths and disasters) of divers famous and renowned Courtezans; The tale of Lol [...]ca Paulina.but especially aboue all the rest, and as a History best agreeing, and paralelling with her owne Fortunes, as also seeking to couer his owne luxurious wickednes, and the wickednesse of his owne coate and calling, vnder a greater mans wantonnesse, he [Page]insisted most vpon the life and fortunes of Lollea Paulina, the greatest Courtezan, the basest Whore, and the deceitfullest Bawde, that euer Rome did acknowledge; But here you must vnderstand, that this subtile Enginer, playd but the flattering Historian, for he only toucht at her felicities, but either forgot, or leapt ouer her infinite miseries; he shewed how she was borne in the City of Fano in Italie, Rome.of good, but poore parentage: how after her Fathers death, necessitty brought her to seeke shelter, being accompanied with nothing but a diuine beauty, an vnmatchable personage, a boundlesse wit, and an infinite spirit. Here Hipolito the great Cardinall of Est, and brother to the Duke of Ferara, beholding her, grew enamored on her, bought her at an infinite price, and maintained her aboue the ranck of the greatest Princes: Insomuch that at an enterview of Ambassadors, before the Pope, in Mounte Angelo (being by torchlight) she came amongst the Ladies so couered, and onercouered with Dyamonds, and the richest Iewelry, that excepting her face, (which the Sun it selfe was not able to darken) there could nothing [Page]be discerned but a mouing flame of most glorious fire; and those which had skill did value her at more then a Million. It is not to be exprest with what greedines of spirit, and earnest delight, Britanica's eares drunke in this Story, for she was infinitly couetous; not as Misers to cram her chests, but as a discreete Prodigall to fill her pockets, placing her delight in giuing, and spending, Ryot, Reuels, Playes, Feasts, and all manner of wastful delights, were to her such deare companions, that rather then loose them, she would lay both life and soule in pawne for their ransome; nay, she was such a true admirer of pleasure, that albe shee knew there was no true pleasure vpon earth, and that only Griefe Went vp & downe, couered in pleasures out-side, yet was her soule so rauisht with delight, that she became dead flesh, and would not feele vexation as long as her eyes stood fixt on Pleasures garments. This Ignatius saw, this he knew, and finding her Spirits take fire at his words, he againe pursued his Story, and shewed her how Paulina hauing nothing but the reuenue of Lustre, a little beautie, was three times the [Page]richest, the greatest and the most respected Lady of all Rome; and here he paul'd, for had he followed the truth of the Story, it would have shewed him that she was three times also the poorest, the most deiected & basest, miserable, despised creature that Rome, or the world had euer reserued for memory: nay, so exceeding hatefull was her condition, & so infinite her contempt, that she gaue her body in prostitution to the common hang-man, for one meale to sustaine her; aud in the end dyed without pitty, and was buried without Ceremonie, had he amplified any thing vpon this Text, he had mar'd his owne market, and made a Conuertite: But they were vvhites beyond his Ayme; confusion was only within the reach of his Arrow, and that he hit, for his wordes did so vvell please her, and his rich promises so assure her, that freely she gaue him her body in possession; and he inioyed her for some short space, vvith out competitor. But Lust is a Gangrene, Her multiplicity of prostitutions.& hauing once poisoned a member, neuer leaues spreading till the vvhole body be cōfounded, she hath now broken one linke in the golden chaine of Chastity, [Page]and she cares not though all the rest be molten with confusion; hence it com's that she entertaines more deuils, variety is pleasant, one ship yelds small custome, great Nauies fil large Treasures, & her reuenues came in with such full Tydes, that false Pleasure made her beleeue there would neuer come ebbing. In this vnchast Progresse, she took an essay or tast of all degrees, all professions, and all conditions, from the Noble, to the Gentile, from the Camp to the City, her eares had heard al languages, her purse had receiued all Coynes, and had not her fortune bin more fauourable then her affections, her body had bin confounded with all manner of putrifactions. But in the end,She determines to turne Bawd looking back into her selfe, and beholding into what a crasie and rotten Barke she had shipt her selfe, on a rough and tempestuous Sea, where infinit diseases lay gaping vpon her, the terrour of the lawes ready to deuoure her, shame and beggerie striuing to ouertake her, and lastly the cursse of all curses, to make her last will on a dunghill, or at the best in a Spittle, this did so perplexe her, that being cloyd with saciety, and sin as weary of her, as [Page]she was weary of sinning, she began to turne ouer the leafe of her wickednesse, and though she had no thought of amendment, yet she wil take a new course for the safety of her health and person. She will no more be a Layis, but a Lena, no more a bewitching Whore, but a deceiuing Bawd, the Sinnes of others shall maintaine her Sinne, she will no more trust her-selfe on the Surges, but wil traffique by Factours, and according to the wealth of her Wares, so shalbe the encrease or decrease of her Reuenues. She had no sooner setled her selfe in this resolution, and sealed it before Hell and her owne evill Conscience, but presently she puts it in execution, and that she might frame all accomodations sutable to her deseigne; she lookes forth for a better house, for her owne wanted elbow roome, she must haue many Meanders, and many passages, many holes, and many hides, deedes of darkenesse doe euer require many darke corners. At last she lights vpon an old ruined [Page]Castell newly repaired, so commodiously placed both for her home-bred customers and Forreine visitants, as well for the Hawke, or Buzzard, that Hyes by day, as the Owle, or Bat, that flutters in the night, that her heart could not wish a place of better convenience; The Sea on one side did beare against the walles, and both tall Shippes, Flyeboates, and Pinnaces, might there Anchor in a safe Harbour; on the other side, it had some sleight intrenchments, which albe they were but weake and assayleable, yet the Sea vpon all assaults, did grant her both Munition, victual, and avoydance. And that a good Name might patronize her many euill actions, she caused it to bee called by a good old Dukes Title.As we say Dukes place Duke Humfrey, or the like. Of this house by contract, she got possession, and her purse being well filled, and wide open, emptied it selfe to giue it adorment, there wanted nothing for State, nothing for Magnificence, nothing for Delight, nothing for Beauty, nothing for Necessity, [Page]howsoeuer the bones that lodg'd in it were rotten and vnwholsome, yet the Monument it selfe was wondrous Gaudie, and hansome; there was nothing now for her to search for, but liuing furniture, and that she diuided into three stations.
